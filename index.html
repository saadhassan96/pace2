About the workshop

Artificial intelligence holds the potential to benefit society in health, the economy, education, and public safety. Sharing those benefits fairly and reducing harm matters, given how technical capacity is concentrated in a few hands, long-standing problems of algorithmic discrimination, transparency, and accountability, and widespread public mistrust of AI.

At Tulane, the Center for Community-Engaged Artificial Intelligence puts community co-creation at the center of how we do research and build computational systems. Our partnerships with New Orleans non-profits and the Tulane Center for Public Service bring community members, students, and researchers together to learn and work side by side.

This workshop covers best practices from the broader research community and what we have learned building systems in partnership with communities to address their needs. Most of the morning is hands-on: you'll bring one problem from your own community-engaged work and think it through with others.
